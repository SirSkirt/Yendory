§ 1 Models

Every model runs on the same Yendory Engine. Nova Bicameral is the default architecture; Nova Neuro is an experimental one you can build from specialised networks. What makes Nova different from Echo is what each one is taught.

Studio looks for a newer build when it starts. Updating installs over this version and keeps your models, datasets, chats and settings.

Unfinished training

Reading and Conversation save a recovery point on an interval and when stopped: the working model with its optimizer state and the run's position (step, random draws, best loss, curriculum stage). Continuing picks the run up where that point was, with the settings it was started with; the accepted model is never replaced by a recovery point. Nothing here continues or is deleted unless you choose it.

§ 2 Reading

Before its lessons, a model can read ordinary text and learn how words and sentences go together (pre-training). One document in ten is held out and never read; a checkpoint is kept only when it predicts those unseen documents better than every earlier one.

Get a corpus

Every source shows its licence. Records are converted into Nova's own format: conversations become U: / N: turns, problems a question with its worked answer. Large sources can be downloaded in part.

Practice generated on this device

Questions with worked answers, computed from each example itself (sums added, little worlds simulated, rules followed), so they are correct by construction. No download, no licence restrictions.

Your own text

Plain .txt, up to 400 MB. Documents are split at <|endoftext|>, or else at blank lines. Text is converted to ASCII.

§ 4 Conversation

After the Classroom, Nova practises real conversations between two people, so it can use what it learned to talk. It practises as a peer, not as an assistant: nothing tells it who to be, and what it thinks comes from what it has read, been taught and said itself.

  • Peers, not assistants. Nothing tells Nova who to be: no persona, system prompt or instruction is put in front of a conversation. Assistant-style datasets (UltraChat, OpenAssistant, LMSYS) are kept as a conversation between two named people, and their conversations in an assistant's style are left out when downloaded. Conversations where someone says they are an AI ("as an AI", "how can I assist you") are left out unless you turn that off below.
  • Quality over quantity. Every downloaded dataset goes through a filter: malformed or broken conversations, duplicates, other languages, unsafe or inappropriate content, links and placeholders, over-long turns, an assistant's style, and anything holding Yendory's own tests are left out. What was kept and why the rest was left out is shown while it downloads and next to the dataset.
  • Every one of Nova's turns. In a conversation, each of Nova's replies is trained, each from the conversation before it, not only the last one.
  • Only Nova's lines are trained. Nova plays one of the two people; the other person's lines are what it answers.
  • Both sides. Each conversation is practised once as each person, so Nova learns to start conversations and ask, not only to answer.
  • Its own name. The person Nova plays is given Nova's name, so it does not learn to call itself by a hundred names.
  • Checked like reading. A conversation has no single right reply, so progress is the loss of Nova's lines in held-out conversations it never practises. A checkpoint must also keep the Classroom answers it had.

Conversations

Every conversation is kept as two named people (speakers without names get names), which lets Nova's speaker take Nova's name. Conversation corpora downloaded on § 2 Reading can be practised too (without names).

How Nova is taught

Each part can be switched off, so a run can show what it changes. Every check reports the same measures either way.

The context tests are pairs of conversations that differ in one earlier fact: a name, where someone went, a fact that changed, a correction, who "she" is, a follow-up, or something never said ("I do not know" is right). A pair passes only when Nova's answer follows the fact in both. They use names, places and things the listening practice never uses, and are never trained on, so they measure whether listening generalises.

The understanding tests check that Nova follows the conversation itself: greeted again (greet back, not "I do not know"), the same question in words she never practised, who said what, the latest of two questions, what was said before and after a change of topic, a misunderstanding put right, and a question asked again. Each is asked two ways: teacher-forced (her earlier turns as the test writes them) and free-running (her earlier turns are the ones she wrote herself, so an early mistake stays in the conversation).

The example conversations are written by hand: thirteen kinds (greetings, how are you, names, remembering, who said what, follow-ups, the same question in other words, the latest message, topic changes, misunderstandings, not knowing, plans, goodbyes), three different conversations of each kind at each level: short exchanges, a few turns and whole conversations. A level is practised once the curriculum reaches it.

Making up your mind

A built-in Classroom course for deciding with reasons: choosing what fits, saying no when there is a reason to, keeping to what it said before, changing its mind for a reason, and keeping its view when someone else has none. Every choice follows from what the question says, never from a preference written into the lessons, so it teaches how to decide, not what to like. Teach it in the Classroom before or between conversation practice.

    § 6 Courses
    
    
    A course is a set of lesson files with pass marks, extra practice material held in reserve, and a final exam. Its grade is 70% lessons (re-graded at the end of the course) and 30% final exam; it passes when the grade reaches the average of its lesson pass marks.
    
    
    File names decide everything: the first number orders the lessons; the words Dense, Transformer or Co-op choose Stage A, B or C; a file named Practice is extra practice for the lesson with the same number and stage (an [Extra Practice] block inside a lesson works too); a file named Exam is the final exam, with [Dense], [Transformer], [Co-op] or [Whole Brain] sections. Answers may list accepted forms and rules, separated by |: animal | creature | !plant (Dense: keywords, a ! word must not appear) or A robin is an animal. | must: robin animal | never: not (sentences). Training uses the first form.

    § 7 Planner
    
    
    A plan trains one model unattended, in order: reading (§ 2), saved courses (§ 3, § 6) and conversation practice (§ 4). Each lesson is taught in its own stage. A failed course gets up to three remediation attempts with different strategies; if it still fails, the plan stops there and the model keeps its best checkpoint. Progress is saved after every lesson, so a stopped plan resumes where it left off.

    § 8 Study
    
    
    Study Mode gives Nova a memory of statements she chose to keep from her reading. It is not part of her network and never answers for her: retrieved memories are put in front of a question, and she has to learn through training how to use them. What she answers without memory (neurally learned) is always measured apart from what she answers with it (remembered).

      § 9 Rewards
      
    
      Yendory grades Nova's answers 0–100% and keeps a reward ledger: one-time credit for new progress on a question, smaller bonuses for answers she keeps across checkpoints, and the most for questions she was never trained on. Reward-guided training uses that history to decide how often each unit is practised; what she is taught never changes. Course final exams never enter the ledger.

      Thinking tree
      
    
      Nova-2 does not write out reasoning steps. For every token, its lower stage runs once and its upper stage runs several thinking rounds, exchanging signals with the dense hemisphere before each round. This tree records that computation while the model answers the question above: the token chosen at each step and the alternatives it weighed, what it would have said after the lower stage and after each round, how much the dense hemisphere changed the decision, and, at the closest calls, where the runner-up would have led. Every value is measured from the model.
